APPLIED RULES (from historical technical analysis):
- Aketon worn under mail only (or as light standalone protection).
- Gambeson for textile protections or worn under brigandine (standard infantry padding).
- Arming Doublet + Mail Voiders: EXCLUSIVE to kits with mobile plate pieces 
  (vambraces, couters, gauntlets, cuisses, tassets, poleyns, greaves, sabatons, pauldrons).
  The Arming Doublet is specifically designed with attachment points (aiglets) for plate.
- War mounts require War Saddle for lance charges.
- Reinforced Gambeson = optimal standalone padded protection (extra layers or leather reinforcements).
- Padded Jack = more rigid, standalone body armor (riveted layers), preferred for heavy infantry without full plate.

TECHNICAL DISTINCTIONS (15th Century Padded Protections):
- AKETON: Fitted, close to body. Originally under mail, later standalone or under cuirass.
- ARMING DOUBLET (Pourpoint d'armement): Key military garment. Fitted, reinforced shoulders/chest, 
  with points/aiglets to attach mobile plate pieces. Priority when using vambraces, cuisses, etc.
- POURPOINT: More civil/elegant term. When military = often synonym of light padded under-armor.
- GAMBESON: Standard bulky quilted protection (multiple linen/wool layers). Best vs blunt trauma. 
  Main choice for pure infantry.
- GAMBESON (LONG / FENDED): Extended to knees/thighs. Common for archers and light infantry.
- REINFORCED GAMBESON: Gambeson with additional padding or leather reinforcements. 
  Best textile-only protection.
- PADDED JACK (Jacque / Jasegrant): Rigid, riveted layers of canvas/leather. 
  Functions as standalone armor. Heavier and stiffer than gambeson.

All equipment names are standardized to match Groupe1_Cote_des_Epees_Equipement_EN.txt.
French role names kept for historical flavor.

HISTORICAL REFINEMENTS APPLIED (June 2026) – Fin XVe siècle fidelity upgrades
================================================================================
The following changes were made to increase historical accuracy for late 15th 
century (Gothic / early Maximilian period) while preserving playability and 
kit economy:

1. Kit 70 "Sergent en maille" : Replaced expensive standalone double-mail hauberk 
   (more mid-15th or tournament style) with Brigandine (85 sp) + kept Aketon 
   as padding. New total: 281 sp. More typical for a late-15th professional 
   sergeant who could not yet afford full plate.

2. Elite high-end kits with redundant high-prestige torso protection (Kit 88 
   "Homme d'armes en brigandine à pied" and Kit 92 "Capitaine de compagnie"): 
   Changed Brigandine (high quality) (240 sp) → Brigandine (85 sp). These kits 
   already have significant limb plate + Armet/Gauntlets; brigandine high quality 
   was historically redundant with a full plate torso. Price reduced accordingly 
   (432 sp and 475 sp). Keeps elite status without anachronistic layering.

3. Polearm-heavy kits (Bill, Halberd, Poleaxe, Bec de Corbin users): Replaced 
   large pavises / Heavy Assault Target with smaller, more mobile shields 
   (Rotella or Great Buckler) or removed where appropriate. 
   Historical note: Late-15th billmen, hallebardiers and poleaxe men in 
   formation (Swiss, Burgundian, English, French companies) rarely carried 
   large pavises – those were mainly for crossbowmen/siege troops or lighter 
   skirmishers. Large shields hinder 2-handed polearm use in tight ranks.
   Affected kits had their totals reduced by 8–17 sp accordingly.

4. Minor price header corrections applied to all affected kits so that listed 
   totals remain accurate.

These refinements keep the overall excellent historical proportions 
(~33% light textile, ~29% brigandine/jack, ~31% partial plate, ~16% heavy 
plate elements, ~8% mounted) while correcting specific late-15th c. details 
(mail role, torso redundancy, shield use with 2H weapons).

The kits remain fully compatible with the Master Equipment file rules 
(24 armor zones, Assault Trot projectile coverage, Dragon Skin option for 
druids, etc.).
