Spell Economic Modifications - 15th Century D&D Campaign
============================================================
Date: June 2026
Context: Low-magic historical campaign set in 15th-century Europe (France-focused)
Goal: Balance powerful utility spells so they do not completely break the medieval economy while remaining useful.

1 gp = 7 grams of pure gold ≈ 7–10 days of wages for a skilled artisan (mason).

============================================================
1. CREATE FOOD AND WATER (Level 3)
============================================================
Original Effect:
- Creates 45 pounds of food and 30 gallons of water.
- Enough to feed 15 humanoids or 5 steeds for 24 hours.
- No material component in base rules.

Proposed Modification:
- Add a material cost of **30 gp per casting** (consumed).
- Suggested component: Ruby dust or a combination of rare spices + a silver coin blessed by a priest.

Economic Reasoning:
- 30 gp ≈ 3 to 4 weeks of wages for a skilled artisan.
- This makes the spell expensive but not impossible to use in emergencies (sieges, long expeditions, famines).
- Prevents casual or repeated use that would crash food prices and devalue agriculture.
- Still allows powerful organizations (armies, rich merchant guilds, cities) to use it strategically.
- Keeps the spell meaningful without making it an everyday solution to hunger.

Verdict: Good balance between utility and economic restraint.

============================================================
2. CREATE OR DESTROY WATER (Level 1)
============================================================
Original Effect:
- Creates or destroys up to 10 gallons (≈ 38 liters) of water.
- No material component in base rules.

Proposed Modification:
- Add a material cost of **10 gp per casting** (consumed).
- Suggested component: A small pearl or a vial of blessed water.

Economic Reasoning:
- 10 gp ≈ 7 to 10 days of wages for a skilled artisan.
- This cost makes the spell useful for important situations (travel, small-scale irrigation, emergencies) but discourages spamming it for large-scale agriculture or city water supply.
- Prevents the spell from becoming a free water source that would heavily impact wells, aqueducts, and water merchants.
- Still allows druids, clerics, or wealthy travelers to use it regularly if needed.

Verdict: Reasonable cost that limits mass adoption while keeping the spell practical.

============================================================
3. CONTINUAL FLAME (Level 2)
============================================================
Original Effect:
- Creates a permanent flame that provides light equivalent to a torch but produces no heat.
- Material component: Ruby dust worth 50 gp (consumed).

Proposed Modification:
- Increase the material cost significantly to **the equivalent of half a modest urban house**.
- From previous economic data: A modest house in Paris during the 15th century was valued around 700–1,500 "francs" (standardized silver value).
- Suggested cost: **400 to 600 gp** in ruby dust or equivalent precious materials.

Economic Reasoning:
- At the original 50 gp cost, Continual Flame is powerful but still somewhat accessible to wealthy individuals and guilds.
- Making it cost the equivalent of half a house turns it into a **major investment**.
- This strongly discourages mass adoption. Only very rich merchants, powerful guilds, cities, or nobles would be able to afford multiple instances.
- Permanent light becomes a status symbol and a strategic resource (mines, important buildings, lighthouses) rather than common technology.
- Prevents the spell from completely destroying the candle, oil, and torch industries.

Verdict: Very dissuasive price. Turns Continual Flame into a rare and prestigious magical infrastructure rather than common utility magic.

============================================================
SUMMARY OF MODIFICATIONS
============================================================

| Spell                    | Level | Original Cost | New Cost per Use     | Economic Goal                              | Risk Level After Change |
|--------------------------|-------|---------------|----------------------|--------------------------------------------|-------------------------|
| Create Food and Water    | 3     | 0 gp          | 30 gp (consumed)     | Prevent food price collapse                | Controlled              |
| Create or Destroy Water  | 1     | 0 gp          | 10 gp (consumed)     | Limit large-scale water production         | Controlled              |

These changes aim to keep magic useful and interesting while preserving the economic structures of a 15th-century setting.

============================================================
NOTES
============================================================
- All costs are consumed material components.
- Prices are calibrated based on 1 gp ≈ 7–10 days of skilled labor wages.
- These modifications can be adjusted further depending on how "low-magic" the campaign is intended to feel.
- Consider roleplaying the components (rare spices, blessed items, precious dusts) to make them feel grounded in the setting.
